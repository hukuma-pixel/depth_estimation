A B S T R A C T
Monocular depth estimation has gained prominence due to its versatile applications and minimal system re-
quirements, despite the inherent challenges posed by its ill-posed nature. Recent advancements in Transformer
techniques have significantly propelled this field forward. This paper introduces a novel Transformer-based
encoder-decoder network to enhance monocular depth estimation, presenting two key improvements
compared to prior approaches. Firstly, a hybrid attention fusion module is designed in the decoding stage for
feature fusion. This module employs a parallel hybrid structure design to fully leverage the complementary
advantages of Transformers and convolution methods, effectively capturing both local contextual and global
consistency of depth maps. Additionally, to mitigate fine-grained depth loss during decoder upsampling, a deep
progressive regression module is proposed. This module gradually predicts depth maps of varying resolutions by
enhancing interactions between adjacent prediction branches. Experimental results on challenging indoor and
outdoor benchmark datasets affirm the superior performance of the proposed method, showing that it effectively
addresses some of the limitations of existing approaches.
1. Introduction
Monocular depth estimation (MDE) endeavors to ascertain the depth
information between the camera and scene objects from a single RGB
image. This depth information finds broad applications in autonomous
driving [1], virtual reality [2], three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction
[3], etc. However, MDE is fundamentally an ill-posed problem due to the
absence of reliable depth cues such as motion and stereo vision re-
lationships, rendering it a challenging subject within the realm of
computer vision.
Current effective methods for MDE heavily depend on deep learning
models to extract intricate information from abundant RGB images [4].
The inception of convolutional neural network (CNN)-based methods
marked a significant milestone in this domain [5–16]. To enhance the
accuracy of CNN-based MDE, several approaches have been explored to
improve the efficacy of convolution operators, including multi-scale
extraction [8], atrous convolution [6,14], channel spatial attention [9,
12,13], and feature pyramid [15]. Achieving precise dense estimation
requires a model with a global receptive field for the scene alongside
local detailed information. However, the inherently local nature of
CNNs poses challenges in capturing long-range global information.
Recognizing the excellence of the Transformer model in modeling
long-term dependencies with global receptive fields [17], researchers
have introduced visual Transformer models for MDE [18–30], yielding
substantial progress. Despite these accomplishments, two primary im-
pediments hinder further advancements in Transformer-based MDE
methods, serving as the motivations for our proposed approach.
Firstly, the majority of Transformer-based MDE methods [18–23]
employ an encoder-decoder architecture. Encoders are typically chosen
from standard image classification backbones pre-trained on ImageNet
[31], and the decoder aggregates multi-scale features to generate the
final depth map. While Transformers excel at modeling long-range de-
pendencies with a global receptive field, CNNs still possess significant
advantages in understanding local contextual information, particularly
considering computational cost. The incorporation of advantages from
both Transformers and CNNs has been demonstrated to effectively
enhance model performance in various vision tasks [32–42]. However,
current methods face challenges in integrating CNNs and Transformers
in the decoder design, resulting in insufficient feature aggregation and a
significant reduction in MDE performance.
Secondly, after the gradual upsampling of decoder features, most
encoder-decoder MDE architectures only leverage the branch of the

To address these challenges, this paper introduces a monocular depth
estimation network with a Hybrid Transformer architecture. The pro-
posed network employs the Swin Transformer [49] as the encoder, and a
novel hybrid attention fusion module (HAFM) is introduced for feature
fusion at each decoder stage. The HAFM leverages both Transformer and
convolution to more effectively capture both local context and global
consistency. Additionally, inspired by the grid-style design, we present a
simple yet effective depth progressive regression module (DPRM) for
estimating multi-scale depth maps using hierarchical features output by
HAFM. HAFM significantly enhances feature interactions between
different prediction branches through two types of skip connections,
effectively preventing the attenuation of fine-grained information dur-
ing the gradual upsampling process. Thanks to these design enhance-
ments, our model demonstrates superior performance compared to
state-of-the-art models.

2. Related work
Deep learning methods have achieved significant strides in MDE,
falling into two primary categories: CNN-based MDE and Transformer-
based MDE. The subsequent sections offer detailed insights into each
category. Additionally, a concise overview of hybrid CNN and Trans-
former models is presented.
2.1. CNN-based MDE methods
Eigen et al. [5] pioneered the application of CNNs to the MDE task,
comprising a global coarse prediction network and a local refinement
network. Subsequently, a continuum of CNN-based MDE methods have
emerged [6–15]. For example, Huynh et al. [12] introduced non-local
coplanarity
constraints
and
non-local
attention
mechanisms
to
enhance the estimation performance of planar structural regions in
depth maps. Both [10] and [11] employed the encoder-decoder archi-
tecture with skip connections for depth map estimation. Li et al. [14]
fused different outputs from the dilated CNN in a hierarchical way to
exploit the multi-scale feature for MDE. Chen et al. [15] applied a CNN
framework with multi-scale feature fusion to generate high-quality
depth predictions. Liu et al. [9] proposed an encoder-decoder with the
combination of multi-scale fusion and joint attention mechanisms,
enhancing the discriminability of feature processing. Lee et al. [8] uti-
lized a local planar guidance operation to guide features to full scale,
deviating from common upsampling layers in the decoding stage.
2.2. Transformer-based MDE methods
The emergence of Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures has
demonstrated their efficiency in obtaining long-range dependencies and
gained state-of-the-art performance for MDE. For example, Ranftl et al.
[20] proposed a universal dense prediction Transformer model that
achieved good results in semantic segmentation and monocular depth
estimation. Bhat et al. [18] refined the output of CNN-based enco-
der-decoder backbone using a ViT with global statistical analysis. Yuan
et al. [19] built a bottom-up-top-down structure with neural window
CRFs as decoder and ViT serves as encoder. Kim et al. [22] utilized ViT
as the encoder and designed a selective feature fusion module to fuse
features in the decoder stage. Li et al. [23] and Shao et al. [25] intro-
duced different adaptive bins strategies based on Swin Transformer
backbone to boost the MDE performance. Ning et al. [24] built an
encoder-decoder depth estimation network based on a novelty trap
attention mechanism.
2.3. The hybrid transformer
Given that Transformer models excel in extracting global de-
pendencies and CNNs possess significant advantages in capturing local
context information, researchers strive to combine both architectures to
achieve complementary benefits and enhance overall model perfor-
mance. Some approaches involve embedding CNNs into the Transformer
architecture. For example, Lee et al. [32] proposed a network named
MPViT, which incorporates convolutional paths within the Transformer

Secondly, we use a progressive regression strategy with efficient skip
connections for depth prediction.
3. Proposed method
This section firstly presents the overall architecture of our network. A
detailed description of the proposed HAFM and DPRM will be provided
in subsequent sections. Finally, we specify our loss function.
3.1. Network architecture
Fig. 1 illustrates the architecture of the proposed encoder-decoder
network. In alignment with recent trends employing the Transformer
for feature extraction, we utilize the Swin Transformer backbone with a
four-stage pyramid structure as the encoder for processing RGB images.

4 C, 8 C}, where C is set to 192 as per the configuration in [49]. In the
decoding stage, the upsampling block (UB) depicted in Fig. 1 is used
between each two adjacent stages to upsample the lower-resolution
feature map for feature fusion. The UB comprises a 3 × 3 convolution
for feature channel reduction, along with a combination of a patch
shuffle layer and a 1 × 1 convolution for feature upsampling.
Three proposed HAFMs fuse the upsampling features with the cor-
responding scale encoder features. The three sets of decoder features
generated by the HAFMs are input to the DPRM. The DPRM progres-
sively predicts three sets of depth maps {D3, D2, D1} with resolutions {H/
4 ×W/4, H/2 ×W/2, H×W}, respectively.
3.2. Hybrid attention fusion module
To efficiently integrate the strengths of both CNN and Transformer
during the hierarchical upsampling of the decoder, we have devised a
HAFM, as depicted in Fig. 2. The HAFM adopts a hybrid Transformer
design. It consists of three types of sub-blocks: the local residual fusion
block (LRFB), the global attention fusion block (GAFB), and the selective
fusion block (SFB), as also illustrated in Fig. 2. The encoder feature and
the upsampling feature are directed to the LRFB and GAFB, respectively.
The GAFB is structured based on a Transformer design, ensuring the
effective fusion of global contextual information, while the LRFB is
designed based on a CNN structure to ensure effective fusion of local
details. Subsequently, the output of the
GAFB and LRFB are fed into the SFB to achieve adaptive fusion of the
global feature and local feature.
As shown in Fig. 2, the GAFB leverages multi-head self-attention
(MHSA) and cross-attention mechanisms to process the input features.

3.3. Depth progressive regression module
In contrast to conventional deep regression methods that exclusively
leverage the branch of the highest-scale feature for regression purposes,
our proposed DPRM adopts a novel approach by predicting three sets of
depth maps with varying resolutions, progressively ranging from small
to large. This progressive depth regression strategy proves advantageous
in mitigating the loss associated with detailed depth information, ulti-
mately yielding more refined depth maps.
As depicted in Fig. 3, the structure of the DPRM is inspired by the
grid-style design [47,48]. To establish three backbone regression
branches, we employ a series connection of an UB and a prediction head
(PH). Subsequently, two types of skip connections are introduced be-

latter, known as the depth-to-feature skip connection, establishes a
connection between the depth map and the input of the PH with an
adjacent scale. This connection is achieved by applying the vertical
block (VB) shown in Fig. 3 to the depth map. The feature-to-feature skip

4.3. Ablation study
Several ablation experiments were undertaken to assess the effec-
tiveness and rationale behind the proposed HAFM and CPRM. All ex-
periments were executed on the NYU-V2 dataset.
4.3.1. Effectiveness of HAFM
Table 4 presents the quantitative comparison results for different
feature fusion methods in the decoder stage. Evidently, the internal
attention mechanisms used by LRFB, GAFB, and SFB make each block
more efficient at processing features, thereby improving predictive
performance. The HAFM combines LRFB, GAFB, and SFB in a
rational manner, resulting in superior depth estimation performance
compared to using any of them individually. Compared to the subopti-
mal result, HAFM improves Abs Rel by 24.5 %, Log10 by 68.4 %, and
RMSE by 26.5 %.
Visual results in Fig. 8 demonstrate the efficacy of our HAFM in
comparison to alternative feature fusion methods. HAFM exhibits a
more comprehensive and superior depth estimation performance. Spe-
cifically, when contrasted with GAFB, HAFM excels in capturing finer
details of object boundaries. Additionally, in comparison to LRFB,
HAFM outperforms in predicting global depth changes.
4.3.2. Effectiveness of DPRM
Table 5 presents quantitative comparisons of various depth regres-
sion methods within the network. The term “one branch” denotes uti-
lizing the branch with the highest-scale fused feature for depth
regression, while “without skip connections” indicates using three
backbone prediction branches without any skip connection for depth
regression. The table illustrates that employing only the highest-scale
feature branch for depth estimation performs the least favorably
among the compared methods. Adding skip connections between adja-
cent prediction branches enhances feature interactions and therefore
significantly improves performance, and the skip connections used in
our DPRM achieve optimal results. In Fig. 9, visual comparisons of
various depth regression methods are presented. Since the performance
indicators of feature-to-feature skip connection and depth-to-feature
skip connection are nearly equal, only the depth-to-feature skip
connection was chosen for qualitative analysis. The visual comparison
results demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed DPRM,
especially in estimating depth maps with finer-grained structural details
of the furniture.
Fig. 10 shows a visual comparison of three different resolution depth
maps (D1, D2, and D3 in Fig. 3) output by DPRM. For convenience, we
adjusted the resolution of each image to the same size. It is evident that
the absolute prediction errors for deep edges in D1 is lower than in D2
and D3, indicating that DPRM effectively mitigates the prediction loss of
fine-grained depth information during the decoder’s layer-by-layer
upsampling process.
5. Conclusion and future work
This paper introduces a Transformer-based MDE network incorpo-
rating hybrid attention fusion and depth progressive regression. The
network includes a HAFM based on a hybrid Transformer design. The
HAFM, rooted in a parallel hybrid Transformer and CNN design, effec-
tively synergizes convolutional and Transformer strengths, elevating
feature fusion within the decoder stage. Additionally, a DPRM is devised
to progressively regress depth maps at different scales. Experimental
results on NYU-V2 and KITTI datasets demonstrated the superior per-
formance of our proposed methods. A series of ablation experiments
validated the efficacy and rationale of the introduced HAFM and DPRM.
Future research endeavors will explore the integration of the proposed
network into a monocular visual simultaneous localization and mapping
system for both indoor and outdoor scenes.

He received his Ph.D. degree from Tianjin University, Tianjin,
China, in 2000. He worked in Ruhr University Bochum of
Germany, Queen’s University of Canada, Heriot-Watt Univer-
sity, University of Leeds, and University of Huddersfield of UK.
His main research interests include 3D optical measurement,
fringe projection profilometry, and phase measuring deflec-
tometry. He has published more than 190 papers. From
2016–2018, he was an EU Marie Curie Individual Fellow in
University of Huddersfield of UK. He is an Associate Editor for
Optics Express now.
